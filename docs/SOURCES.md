## Module 8 — medication log

Read on 14 November 2026. Official NASA, NIH and FDA documents. The module **does not prescribe**: it logs what the crew member took, follows up on whether it worked and flags patterns worth raising with the crew medical officer. It never suggests a medication or a dose and never interprets a symptom.

- **NASA ISS medical kit list** — "3.001 Medical Kit – Contents and Reference" (MED CL/SpX-6, 09 APR 15), NASA public release. Gives the names the log offers, the kit categories (Pain Reliever, Sleep, Decongestant, Space Motion Sickness/Anti-Nausea, Steroid, Stool Softener — the table headings), the possible side effects the follow-up asks about, and the comments the warnings quote. Table cells are quoted word for word with line breaks removed.
- **NASA HRP evidence report "Risk Factor of Inadequate Nutrition"** (Smith, Zwart, Heer; approved for release 5 Jan 2015), https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/evidence/reports/Nutrition.pdf — vitamin D in the mission plan (pp. 54–56 and 122–123). Vitamins C, E and B were proposed by the team pharmacist and left out: the same report treats them as open questions (vitamin C p. 69, vitamin E p. 58, folate and B12 linked to vision changes p. 168), not as supplements to take.
- **NASA OCHMO technical briefs, NASA HRP and FDA labels / NIH NCCIH** — kept for the label data, the medications' performance cautions used by Fit for duty, and context. The screen no longer shows label directions or doses.

### Open points for the pharmacist

- **Acetaminophen 325 mg:** the NASA kit list says "No more than 12 tablets every 24 hours."; the current FDA label says 10. The log's warning quotes the kit list (12).
- **The kit list is from 2015;** the current ISS formulary may differ.
- **Vitamin D:** NASA's report gives 800 IU a day as the recommendation (2015). The app says "every day" and quotes the report; it does not present a dose as current.
- **Side effects** in the follow-up are the kit list's "Possible Side Effects" column, split at the commas.

### App rules in this module (illustrative, not NASA limits)

When "Did it work?" is asked after each medication · the same kit category logged on 3 days in a row → "Include it in the next downlink to Earth" · a medication that did not work twice in a row → "Talk it over with your crew medical officer" · the body zones that say what a dose was for · the alarm-sign list. Warnings that quote the kit list: more than its 24-hour limit, more days in a row than it allows, and sleep medications combined within 24 hours.
